Our Story

About Aurelia Bay

A forty-room resort on the Konkan coast, built slowly, on purpose, by people who'd rather get it right than get it fast.

How it started

Aurelia Bay opened with twelve rooms and a single idea: that a resort could feel like a well-kept home rather than a hotel. The land had belonged to a coconut farming family for three generations before the first foundation went in, and much of the original grove is still standing between the rooms today.

Two decades on, the property has grown to forty keys, a working kitchen garden, and a small spa — but the family who started it still walks the grounds most mornings, and the rule that got the resort built hasn't changed: nothing goes up that blocks a neighbor's view of the water.

What We Believe

Three ideas we don't compromise on

Built for the coastline

Every room was placed to keep a sightline to the water and to stay clear of the tide line — no exceptions, even when it costs us a room.

Slow, not empty

There's plenty to do — diving, the spa, the kitchen garden tours — but nothing is scheduled for you. The pace is yours to set.

Local first

Most of what's on your plate was grown or caught within a few kilometers, and most of the people serving it grew up in the village next door.
